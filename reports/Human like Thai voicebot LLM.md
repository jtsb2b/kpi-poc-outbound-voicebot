# Make the Thai voicebot listen like a person

The KPI bot will sound most human by fixing what it hears and when it speaks, not by giving the LLM more freedom to talk. The observed failures are all failures of interpretation and turn management, not of wording: "ถั่วต้ม" and "มั้งครับ" accepted as confirmation, "มีครับ" treated as a question, "ขออีกทีครับ" replaying a whole unit, the same "ค่ะ" every turn, long monologues. The deterministic state machine should stay in charge. Qwen's job should widen from yes/no/other classification to a small, fixed vocabulary of conversation-repair commands (repeat, unclear, has-question, wait, hedged-yes, correction) that the flow handles explicitly. Human-like timing comes from hiding latency, not removing it. Human turn gaps are around 200 ms, and a cascade over SIP with this stack naively adds up to roughly 1.3–2.3 s. Getting near the ~800 ms practitioner target requires three things: speculative interpretation during the endpointing silence, pre-synthesised audio for every template line, and Thai-aware endpointing and barge-in. LiveKit 1.8.x does not provide the last one out of the box, because its turn-detector models do not support Thai and its word-count interruption filter counts Thai characters as words. The Thai surface also has to be engineered: one polite particle per turn instead of one per clause, คะ on questions and ค่ะ on statements, a rotating acknowledgement pool chosen by dialogue function, all numbers normalised to Thai words before TTS, and a single fixed Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS voice with a short, stable style string. Measure success with percentile voice-to-voice latency, false-barge-in and takeover rates, slot and intent accuracy on a regression set of real Thai utterances, and native-Thai listener ratings. Neither an LLM judge nor vendor dashboards alone are enough.

## An 800 ms gap is achievable only by hiding work, not by faster parts

Humans leave very small gaps between turns. Levinson and Torreira put typical gaps "of the order of 200 ms", while producing speech takes over 600 ms. That means listeners plan their reply while the other person is still talking ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full)). Across ten languages, average gaps differ by no more than about 250 ms from the cross-language mean ([MPI](https://forms.mpi.nl/node/50939)). No Thai turn-gap measurement exists in the literature reviewed. Practitioners building cascades recommend aiming for **about 800 ms voice-to-voice** ([AI Engineer talk](https://ai.engineer/talks/pipecat-cloud-enterprise-voice-agents-built-on-open-source)). Vendors warn that gaps **above about 800 ms make PSTN callers think the line dropped** ([Coval](https://www.coval.dev/blog/voice-ai-latency)). These vendor bands disagree with each other and none publish their data, so treat them as orientation only.

Adding up this stack's stages shows the problem. LiveKit's default endpointing wait alone is `min_delay=0.5` s ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/turn.py)). Qwen interpretation is measured at about 0.35–0.85 s. Gemini Flash TTS has no published time-to-first-audio, and forum reports range from under 2 s to 10–20 s depending on key and project ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/3-1-flash-tts-preview-streaming-latency/176050)). The SIP legs add tens to hundreds of milliseconds each way ([Parloa](https://www.parloa.com/knowledge-hub/factors-affecting-latency/)). Run in series, that is well over a second before the bot says anything, even before live TTS. The rest of this section covers each place the delay can be hidden.

| Stage | Naive cost on this stack | Technique that hides it |
|---|---|---|
| Endpointing silence | 0.5 s default, up to 3.0 s max | Per-state `min_delay`, dynamic mode, Thai particle end-of-turn cue |
| Qwen interpretation | ~0.35–0.85 s | Preemptive (speculative) run during the endpointing silence; fewer output tokens |
| TTS first audio | Unknown, possibly seconds | Pre-synthesised template audio via `session.say(text, audio=...)` |
| Perceived gap remaining | — | Conditional cached acknowledgement only if the reply isn't ready after ~300–400 ms |

### Endpointing is the largest controllable delay, and Thai gets no model help

In 1.8.x all turn settings live under `AgentSession(turn_handling={...})`. The old `min_endpointing_delay` and `max_endpointing_delay` arguments are deprecated aliases ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_session.py)). **Neither the Hugging Face multilingual turn detector nor the local `v1-mini` language table includes Thai** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/livekit/turn-detector); [source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/tree/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/inference/eot)). For an unsupported language the framework logs a warning, skips the model, and commits the turn on the delay alone. Adding the turn detector for Thai therefore buys nothing. Use `turn_detection="stt"` if the internal ASR emits fast, reliable finals. Otherwise use `"vad"`. Set `endpointing={"mode": "dynamic", ...}` so that `min_delay` becomes an exponential moving average of this caller's own pauses ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/endpointing.py)). Override it per `Agent`/state. A reasonable starting point, to be tuned on real calls, is about 0.3 s after a yes/no question and 0.7–1.0 s after open questions such as "why did you give that score". Full-Duplex-Bench treats pauses of 0.4–1.0 s inside a user turn as normal, so the bot must not take over in that window ([arXiv 2503.04721](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04721v3)).

There is also a Thai-specific cue to exploit. Sentence-final particles (ครับ, ค่ะ, คะ, นะคะ) strongly signal end of turn. The public `_TurnDetector` protocol in `voice/turn.py` (`predict_end_of_turn`, `supports_language`, `unlikely_threshold`) is small enough to implement as a rule. That rule would return a high end-of-turn probability when the interim transcript ends in a particle and a low one when it ends in a conjunction such as แล้วก็ or คือ. No one has published this, so A/B test it.

### Run interpretation speculatively, but make it side-effect free and quota-aware

Preemptive generation is **on by default** in 1.8.x. It starts the LLM on a final or preflight transcript before end-of-turn is confirmed, and uses the result immediately if nothing changed ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/turn.py)). There are three catches for a deterministic flow. First, speculation runs the agent's `llm_node`, and the result is **discarded if `on_user_turn_completed` changes chat context or tools** ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_activity.py)). Place the interpretation call, the state transition, and template selection inside a custom `llm_node`, and inject state there rather than in `on_user_turn_completed`. Second, each turn can be speculated up to `max_retries=3` times. The node must therefore compute the next state without committing it, and commit only once the speech is actually scheduled, or key transitions by turn ID so they are idempotent. Third, the token quota. At ~1k tokens per interpretation, 60k tokens/min allows about 60 interpretations per minute. Assuming one customer turn every ~10 s per call, that supports roughly 10 concurrent calls without speculation, but only 2–3 at worst-case speculation. Set `max_retries=1`, or raise the quota, before turning this on at scale. It also only works if the internal ASR emits finals or `PREFLIGHT_TRANSCRIPT` events. Plugins that do not emit preflight events never trigger it ([issue #4317](https://github.com/livekit/agents/issues/4317)).

### Take TTS off the critical path and shrink the LLM's output

Every bot line is a template, so pre-render them. `session.say(text, audio=frames)` plays supplied frames and still records the text in chat context and transcript ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_session.py)). Split slotted lines into static segments plus a slot. Cache common slot values (Thai numbers, months, plan names). Synthesise the customer's name at call setup, since an outbound call knows it in advance. Key the cache by text, voice, style and model version so that switching to the internal TTS invalidates it. Splice seams between cached segments were not studied, so listen for them.

On the vLLM side, measured time to first byte is ~0.14 s against a ~0.5 s total, so decode dominates. Fewer output tokens is the main lever. Use short enum fields, no free-text "reason", and a `max_tokens` sized to the schema. XGrammar's per-token overhead is negligible: 6.2 → 6.3 ms TPOT at batch 1 ([arXiv 2411.15100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100)). Schema compilation does cost time-to-first-token, though, so reuse byte-identical schemas per state. Prefix caching only cuts prefill ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/automatic_prefix_caching.html)). Order the prompt static-first: system rules, then the schema and examples, then state instructions, then history, then the latest utterance. Never put call IDs or timestamps at the top. Run classification at temperature 0 and pass `chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": false}` per request. Some Qwen deployments reportedly ignored this flag, so verify it on your version ([vLLM issue 35574](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35574)).

If the bot ever streams generated text into TTS, note that LiveKit's sentence splitter only breaks on `. ! ? 。！？` ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/main/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/tokenize/_basic_sent.py)). Unpunctuated Thai then reaches TTS as one sentence and first audio waits for the whole reply. Write a Thai tokenizer that flushes at a space after a particle once at least ~15 Thai characters have accumulated and no number or date span is open.

### Use acknowledgement-first only as a conditional filler

Fillers measurably improve perceived wait time, most when delays are long. Context-appropriate fillers beat generic ones ([arXiv 2507.22352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22352); [arXiv 2508.11781](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11781v1)). But they "do not mitigate its broader effects on user experience". The Alexa Prize data found fillers helped user ratings but not external listeners' ratings ([ACL W19-5935](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/W19-5935)). Play a cached neutral token ("ค่ะ") only when the real reply is still not ready ~300–400 ms after end of turn. Never play one that presupposes the interpretation (for example "ขอบคุณค่ะ" before you know the customer refused). Make it interruptible and render it in the same Gemini voice.

## Thai backchannels break LiveKit's default barge-in, so gate by dialogue state

A Thai customer listening to a policy readout will say "ค่ะ", "ครับ", "อือ" or "ใช่" as continuers. The same tokens are answers once the bot asks a question. LiveKit's defaults handle this poorly on a self-hosted Thai SIP stack for three reasons. First, the ML **adaptive interruption model runs only on LiveKit Cloud inference**. With `mode` unset in self-hosted production, the source logs "adaptive interruption is disabled by default in production mode" and falls back to VAD ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_activity.py)). Forcing it on would send call audio to LiveKit Cloud, which is a data-residency question for insurance. Its vendor accuracy claim (86% precision, 100% recall at 500 ms) has no per-language or telephony breakdown ([LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/adaptive-interruption-handling)). Second, `min_words` splits Thai with `split_character=True`, so **"ค่ะ" counts as three words** and passes any realistic threshold ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/tokenize/_basic_word.py)). Third, `aec_warmup_duration`, which ignores early barge-ins while echo cancellation settles, defaults to **`None` for outbound SIP** ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_session.py)). Line echo and TV noise therefore reach VAD directly. Krisp's 8 kHz `BVCTelephony` requires LiveKit Cloud transport, and self-hosters are pointed to ai-coustics under a separate licence ([field guide](https://livekit.com/field-guides/guide/noise-cancellation-agents-sip); [community](https://community.livekit.io/t/questions-on-recording-noise-cancellation-in-self-hosted-livekit-setup/968)).

The workable design follows Vapi's `acknowledgementPhrases` and Pipecat's buffered `MinWordsInterruptionStrategy` ([Vapi](https://docs.vapi.ai/customization/voice-pipeline-configuration); [Pipecat](https://docs.pipecat.ai/server/utilities/interruption-strategies)), adapted to Thai: a **state-dependent lexicon gate**. While the bot reads a fact, a transcript made up only of continuer tokens (ค่ะ, คะ, ครับ, อือ, อืม, อ๋อ, ใช่, โอเค, ได้, เออ, ฮะ, จ้ะ, possibly repeated) does not count as an interruption. After the bot asks a question, the same tokens are never suppressed, because they are the answer. A community report that adaptive handling "ignores single word answers" ([thread](https://community.livekit.io/t/adaptive-turn-handling-ignores-single-word-answers/1104)) is exactly the failure this ordering prevents. Wannaruk's Thai/American telephone study found that brief restatements signal agreement in Thai ([IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/81503)). An echoed slot value ("ตุลาคมนะ") during a readout should therefore also be treated as a continuer, not a correction, unless it contradicts the read value.

Sensible starting values, inferred and to be tuned on the real carrier, are: `min_duration` 0.6–0.8 s while reading facts (the default 0.5 s is about the length of a "ค่ะ"), `false_interruption_timeout` 1.0–1.5 s instead of 2.0 s (two seconds of dead air on a phone feels broken), and `resume_false_interruption=True`. When pausing is possible, a VAD barge-in pauses audio rather than cancelling it, and playback resumes if no transcript arrives before the timeout ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_activity.py)). Whether `can_pause` is true on the self-hosted SIP output path was not verified. **Decide which layer owns resume.** If both LiveKit's resume and the project's segment-resume logic act, the customer hears a double resume.

### Resume at the fact boundary, never mid-sentence and never the whole monologue

Interrupted speech is recorded honestly. `PlaybackFinishedEvent.synchronized_transcript` and the truncated chat history keep only what was heard ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/io.py)). Gemini TTS likely provides no word timestamps, so treat the cut point as approximate and snap to segment boundaries. Conversation analysis shows humans recycle the *start* of a turn after overlap ([Schegloff](https://conversationanalysis.org/mp-files/recycled-turn-beginnings.pdf)), and Schegloff explicitly excludes continuers from competitive overlap ([EMCA wiki](https://emcawiki.net/Overlap)). That gives a policy. After a real interruption with content, answer it, then restart the *interrupted fact* (a half-heard fact is not confirmed), skipping facts already fully heard. After a false interruption, resume silently, or with "ต่อนะคะ" if the pause exceeded ~1 s. After a second interruption on the same fact, ask "ให้ทวนส่วนนี้อีกครั้งไหมคะ" instead of replaying. This only feels natural if each fact is one short sentence. The resume unit and the monologue problem have the same fix.

Do not have the bot produce its own backchannels while the customer gives open feedback in this PoC. The evidence is that misplaced or consecutive artificial backchannels are judged inappropriate ([Buschmeier & Kopp](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/perceptual-evaluation-of-backchannel-strategies-for-artificial-li/); [arXiv 2410.15929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15929)). On a half-duplex SIP cascade the bot's own "ค่ะ" can echo into ASR or make the customer stop talking. LiveKit computes a `backchannel_probability` but its consumer is a TODO ([source](https://github.com/livekit/agents/blob/livekit-agents%401.8.5/livekit-agents/livekit/agents/voice/agent_activity.py)). Put a short acknowledgement at the start of the bot's next turn instead.

## The interpreter needs repair commands and an abstain option, not more confidence

Each observed failure has the same root cause: the schema let Qwen pick only among answer intents, so nonsense, hedges and meta-utterances were forced into "yes" or "question". Rasa CALM is the most explicit published version of the "LLM understands, flow decides" pattern. Its LLM emits 1–3 commands from a fixed vocabulary (SetSlot, Clarify, ChitChat, CannotHandle, HumanHandoff, Cancel…) that execute deterministically. On the authors' own 71-conversation benchmark it passed **95.8% vs 47.9%** for an intent baseline, including 13/13 corrections ([arXiv 2402.12234](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12234)). Its "patterns" are meta-flows for correction, cancel, repeat, clarify, silence and cannot-handle ([Rasa patterns](https://rasa.com/docs/reference/primitives/patterns/)). Notably, **Rasa's default repeat pattern replays every bot message since the last user turn**, and its own docs call that a poor fit for long responses. That is the "ขออีกทีครับ" failure, reproduced by the leading framework's default.

Add a small set of always-available repair commands alongside the per-state answer intents. A concrete schema shape, with classification fields first so they decode before anything optional (ARQ-style ordering improved adherence in [arXiv 2503.03669](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03669)):

```json
{
  "act": "answer | hedged_answer | unclear | repeat_request | has_question | question | wait | correction | opt_out | off_topic",
  "value": "<per-state enum, e.g. yes | no | score_1..score_5 | null>",
  "question_text": "<only when act=question>",
  "ack": "<index into approved acknowledgement pool, or null>"
}
```

The flow then maps each failure to a deterministic response. **"ถั่วต้ม"** becomes `unclear`, which must exist in every state's enum and can **never map to yes**. Re-ask the short form once ("ขอโทษค่ะ ข้อมูลถูกต้องไหมคะ"), and on the second failure mark the item unanswered or escalate. For consequential confirmations, add a cheap deterministic corroboration on top: a `yes` must contain an affirmative token (ใช่, ถูก, ถูกต้อง, ครับ/ค่ะ alone, ได้, โอเค, ค่ะใช่). This is an engineering inference, not a published method. **"มั้งครับ"** (and น่าจะ, คิดว่า, มั้ง, ไม่แน่ใจ) becomes `hedged_answer`. Classic spoken-dialogue practice uses confidence tiers: reject and re-ask when low, confirm explicitly when middling, confirm implicitly when high ([Pearl](https://www.ida.liu.se/~arnjo82/chi-ws-02/papers/pearl.doc)). Rejection thresholds should differ per state according to the cost of an error ([Bohus & Rudnicky](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2005/bohus05_interspeech.html)). A hedge on policy confirmation therefore triggers one explicit check ("ขอยืนยันอีกครั้งนะคะ ว่า…ถูกต้องไหมคะ"). A hedge on a satisfaction score can be accepted with an implicit echo. **"มีครับ"** in reply to "มีคำถามเพิ่มเติมไหมคะ" is an *answer* (yes, I have one), not a question. It becomes `has_question`, and the bot says "เชิญสอบถามได้เลยค่ะ" and waits with a longer endpointing delay. Log a follow-up task only when `act=question` carries actual `question_text` that the flow cannot answer. **"ขออีกทีครับ"** becomes `repeat_request`. Replay only the current item's authored **short form**. On a second repeat, use a simpler variant, optionally slower. If the current unit is long, ask which part the customer missed.

Do not trust a self-reported confidence field to make these calls. Verbalised LLM confidence clusters at 80–100% and separates right from wrong poorly: even GPT-4 had an AUROC of about 62.7% ([arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13063)). LLMs also rarely abstain unless the option is structurally present. Put safety in the structure instead: per-state enums, a mandatory abstain label, deterministic corroboration for high-impact acts (opt-out, complaint, consent withdrawal), and, if vLLM exposes them, logprobs on the `act` token calibrated against labelled Thai calls.

### Generate only a slot inside a fixed line, and verify it

To make bot lines sound less canned without letting the LLM restate facts, follow the narrowest documented pattern. Rasa's rephraser rewrites whole templates, is opt-in per response, and is documented to "change a message's meaning". Rasa ships no fact-preservation check ([Rasa rephraser](https://rasa.com/docs/reference/primitives/contextual-response-rephraser/)). The NLG literature quantifies the risk. Even the best SC-GPT generator's slot error rate rose from **1.09% to 4.96%** on unseen dialogue acts ([SC-GPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.12328)). Parlant's "strict" mode, where the LLM only selects among approved templates and a template referencing a field is unusable unless that field was supplied, is the closest fit for a regulated line ([Parlant](https://www.parlant.io/docs/concepts/customization/canned-responses)). In practice, regulated content (AI disclosure, recording notice, policy number, premium, coverage dates, consent) stays as fixed, pre-synthesised `say()` audio. Variety comes from a generated or selected prefix: the acknowledgement, a short empathy clause after a low score, or a transition. That prefix is chosen from a compliance-reviewed pool by index, which is what the `ack` field above does. If free text is ever allowed, run a post-check before speaking: every required literal is present, no digits or amounts are absent from the slot set, a length cap holds, and no banned commitments (รับประกัน, promises of callback or compensation) appear. On failure, fall back to the template.

### Kill monologues at authoring time

Vendor prompting guides converge on short turns and explicit variety rules, and note that models copy sample phrases closely ([OpenAI Realtime guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/realtime_prompting_guide)). For a template bot the fix is editorial. Author each policy detail as its own one-sentence segment of about 4–7 s spoken (12 s hard cap). Ask one question per turn. Insert a check-in after every two or three facts rather than at the end. Use the short product name ("แผนพีเอ เต็มใจ") after first mention. This also makes the barge-in resume unit small and the repeat short form easy to write.

## Natural Thai depends on particle placement, register and pre-normalised numbers

The Royal Institute notes that Thai speech without a final particle sounds ห้วน and กระด้าง (curt, harsh) ([ORST, via search summary](https://www.orst.go.th/FILEROOM/CABROYINWEB/DRAWER004/GENERAL/DATA0000/00000640.FLP/html/244/)). Learner guides warn that over-use sounds stiff ([ThaiPod101](https://www.thaipod101.com/lesson/thai-teachers-answer-your-questions-10-how-does-formal-thai-work?lp=144)). The monotonous-"ค่ะ" complaint is the over-use failure. No Thai call-centre corpus quantifies the right density, so the following guidance is native-speaker practice that KPI's QA team should confirm. Put one particle at the end of the turn's final clause, not after each clause. Use **คะ on questions, ค่ะ on statements, นะคะ to soften requests**. The written choice between the two is error-prone even among Thai writers ([TCI](https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/abc/article/view/237206)), and a wrong spelling becomes a wrong tone in TTS. Add a lint that flips a sentence ending in ค่ะ to คะ when it contains a question word (ไหม, หรือเปล่า, อะไร, เมื่อไร, กี่, ยังไง), and that rejects "นะค่ะ". Use ดิฉัน or the persona name once in the AI disclosure, then drop the first-person pronoun. Address the customer as คุณ + first name at greeting and verification, and sparingly after that. Avoid written-register connectives (ทั้งนี้, ดังกล่าว, ดำเนินการ, โปรดทราบว่า), which sound read aloud.

Acknowledgements should be selected by **function**, rotated with no repeat within the last few turns, and kept as separate pre-cached clips:

| Function | Approved pool (verify with QA) | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Plain receipt | ค่ะ · ค่ะ ๆ | Mid-flow neutral answers |
| Compliance | ได้ค่ะ · ได้เลยค่ะ | Customer agrees to a request or asks to wait |
| Understanding | เข้าใจค่ะ · เข้าใจแล้วค่ะ | After an explanation or reason |
| Realisation | อ๋อ ค่ะ | After a correction or clarification |
| Implicit confirm | echo of the value + ค่ะ ("ตุลาคม ค่ะ") | After a slot answer (fits Thai restatement-as-agreement, [Wannaruk](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/81503)) |
| Empathy | เข้าใจเลยค่ะ · ขอโทษด้วยนะคะที่… | After a low satisfaction score or complaint |
| Thanks | ขอบคุณค่ะ · ขอบคุณสำหรับความคิดเห็นค่ะ | Only after the interpretation is known |

"รับทราบค่ะ" is bureaucratic if used every turn. "ขออนุญาต" more than once or twice per call sounds scripted.

### Normalise every number to Thai words before Gemini sees it

No source tested Gemini TTS's handling of Thai numbers, dates or abbreviations. Pre-normalising removes the uncertainty and makes the later switch to internal TTS seamless. PyThaiNLP provides `num_to_thaiword`, `bahttext`, `thai_strftime`, `time_to_thaiword`, `maiyamok` and `abbreviation_to_full_text` ([PyThaiNLP util](https://pythainlp.org/docs/5.0/api/util.html)). Apply them in a fixed order:

1. Protect phone numbers, policy numbers and IDs, read digit by digit as grouped (หนึ่ง, never เอ็ด).
2. Convert times matching `\d{1,2}[.:]\d{2}\s*น\.` to นาฬิกา.
3. Convert dates, with Buddhist-era years read either in full or digit by digit, consistently, and พ.ศ. read as พอศอ.
4. Convert money; consider stripping bahttext's receipt-like "ถ้วน".
5. Convert remaining cardinals, where a final 1 in multi-digit numbers is เอ็ด ([Trueplookpanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/learning/detail/31878-044467)).
6. Expand abbreviations.
7. Expand ๆ.
8. Transliterate Latin acronyms (AI → เอไอ, PA → พีเอ).

Plain whitespace sentence splitting breaks dates and amounts ([PyThaiNLP tokenize](https://pythainlp.org/docs/5.0/api/tokenize.html)), so normalise before you chunk.

### Pin one Gemini 3.8 Flash voice and keep the style string short and constant

**Thai is supported by `gemini-3.8-flash-tts` but not by Flash-Lite TTS.** Streaming returns headerless 24 kHz mono 16-bit PCM, and turn-wide delivery goes in `speech_metadata.style`, while the text field is strictly the transcript ([Gemini API](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation)). Google states that long "Audio Profile"/"Director's Notes" prompts are **the most common cause of voice drift**. The recommended fix is a Voice-design custom voice reused by ID with a minimal style. Female library options include Sulafat ("Warm"), Vindemiatrix ("Gentle"), Despina ("Smooth") and Erinome ("Clear"). Users report that 3.8 still makes random tonal jumps, usually right after a sentence boundary, an issue Google escalated without resolving ([forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-8-tts-voice-consistency-issue/186103)). On 3.1, voice identity drifted between separate requests ([forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-1-flash-tts-preview-voice-identity-changes-between-separate-requests/179498)). Pre-rendering is therefore also a quality control: generate each clip, screen it with a speaker-embedding similarity check against a reference, and regenerate outliers offline instead of gambling live.

Use a fixed style such as "warm, polite and unhurried". LiveKit's 3.1 testing found that "quiet", "flat", "monotone" and "no rush" collapsed prosody, and that custom emotion tags gave flatter delivery ([LiveKit guide](https://livekit.com/blog/gemini-3.1-flash-tts-prompting-guide)). For pauses, the 3.8 Gemini API documents `<short pause>` and `<long pause>` in angle brackets. Cloud TTS docs for 2.5/3.1 use square brackets and warn that untested tags can be spoken aloud ([Cloud TTS](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)). A bare `<pause>` is not documented for either. Use tags sparingly: between phone-number groups and before a key figure, with text between any two tags. Prefer commas over full stops between clauses, since period-separated fragments sound choppy. Verify on Thai that no tag is vocalised. Cloud limits of ≤4,000 bytes of text work out to about 1,300 Thai characters, and temperature is ignored. Finally, test digit and tone intelligibility after resampling 24 kHz down to 8 kHz G.711.

## Human-likeness must be measured at the SIP edge and by native Thai ears

No standard sets voice-agent targets, and vendor thresholds contradict each other. One vendor gives p99 latency targets of both 8 s and 4 s on different pages ([Hamming article](https://hamming.ai/blog/how-to-evaluate-voice-agents-2026); [Hamming methodology](https://hamming.ai/methodology)). Set internal targets and measure them where the customer hears them. Measure voice-to-voice latency at the SIP leg, not by summing component timings. One LiveKit user saw component totals under 1 s but 3–4 s from end of speech to reply ([community](https://community.livekit.io/t/high-end-to-end-latency-asr-llm-tts-total-1s-but-speech-end-to-reply-takes-3-4s-livekit-agents/1277)). LiveKit's `end_of_turn_delay` and `transcription_delay` show where the time goes, though one release misreported transcription delay under STT turn detection ([PR 4396](https://github.com/livekit/agents/pull/4396)), so check your SDK version. Reasonable PoC targets synthesised from the evidence: voice-to-voice p50 ≈ 1.0 s and p90 ≤ 2.5 s, barge-in stop ≤ 200–300 ms, false barge-in ≤ 5%, zero takeovers in 0.4–1.0 s customer pauses, and dead-air > 2 s counted per call.

Before audio exists, the text simulator should enforce the behaviours that failed. Turn this into a CI regression set of real Thai utterances with expected `act`/`value` per state, beginning with ถั่วต้ม, มั้งครับ, น่าจะครับ, มีครับ, ขออีกทีครับ, เดี๋ยวนะคะ, and complaints that sound like opt-outs. Add deterministic checks: estimated spoken seconds per bot turn against the 4–7 s target and 12 s cap, questions per turn, the คะ/ค่ะ lint, acknowledgement repetition within N turns, name mentions per three turns, and slot accuracy. For audio, borrow Full-Duplex-Bench's probes ([arXiv 2503.04721](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04721v3)), re-recorded in Thai over the real carrier:

1. Mid-sentence pauses, to measure takeover.
2. ค่ะ/ครับ/อืม played during readouts, to measure false stops and resumes.
3. Real barge-ins, to measure stop latency and whether the next turn addresses them.
4. Mid-call corrections.

Use LLM judges only for subjective items, and calibrate them first. Strong judges match human-human agreement (>80%) on pairwise preference but show position and verbosity bias ([NeurIPS 2023](https://nips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73434)). In a speech benchmark the LLM judge's length bias was +17.3% vs +12.9% for humans ([MTalk-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.18240)). That bias pushes directly against short turns. Use a written Thai rubric (particles, register, address, one question per turn, empathy without stock repetition, truthful AI disclosure), score in both orders, penalise length explicitly, and use a judge from a different model family than Qwen. Accept the judge only after its agreement with 2–3 Thai QA raters on ~100 conversations approaches human-human agreement. LiveKit's pytest `JudgeGroup` with `accuracy_judge` is the cheapest harness ([LiveKit evals](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/python/livekit/agents/evals/index.html)).

For perceived voice quality, use P.808-style 1–5 ratings with **at least ~60 votes per condition**, where correlation with lab MOS plateaus ([arXiv 2003.11300](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.11300)). Use CMOS A/B tests to compare two style strings or voices. Raters must be native Thai speakers: the lowest crowd-lab agreement in that study was a German crowd rating Swiss-German speech. For whole-call human-likeness there is no validated Thai instrument. A forced-choice "human or bot?" on 20–40 s excerpts mixed with real KPI agent recordings, plus translated Godspeed anthropomorphism/likeability items ([HRI scale database](https://hriscaledatabase.psychology.gmu.edu/attitudes/attributes/2024/10/31/Godspeed.html)), gives a defensible, if unvalidated, number to track over time.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that "human-like" for this bot is mostly about *listening*. Turning on LLM rephrasing or free generation would add risk in a regulated domain and would fix none of the observed failures. Each failure traces to a missing repair category in the interpreter's output space, or to turn-taking defaults built for English on LiveKit Cloud. Two consequences for the build plan follow. First, the biggest naturalness gains are cheap, deterministic and testable in the existing text simulator: schema changes, short-form templates, acknowledgement pools and a particle lint. Second, the hardest remaining problem is not Qwen but the self-hosted Thai SIP audio path. Thai endpointing, Thai backchannel gating, echo with no AEC warmup, and Gemini TTS latency and drift each have no off-the-shelf answer and need measurement on the real carrier.

Several points remain uncertain and should be checked early. The internal ASR's end-of-utterance and preflight contract determines whether `"stt"` turn detection and speculative interpretation are possible at all. Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS time-to-first-audio and its Thai number reading are unpublished. The 60k tokens/min quota, more than model speed, may cap concurrency once speculation is on. The Thai linguistic guidance (particle density, acknowledgement pool, number-reading conventions) is native-speaker practice, not corpus evidence. KPI's own QA scorecards and recorded agent calls are the best available ground truth and should be used to validate it.
